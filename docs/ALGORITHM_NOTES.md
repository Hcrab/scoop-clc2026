# Algorithm assumptions and evaluation

This is a synthetic, fixed-horizon prototype. No ground-truth event table is queried by forecasting, monitoring, investigation, or planning. The offline evaluator alone reads planted-event labels. The investigator is a deterministic checklist and the narrator is scripted; neither is an LLM.

## Forecast and information cutoff

The Poisson LightGBM output is an expected mean, not p50. Daily p10/p90 are separately fitted quantile models. Store/SKU means add coherently; daily quantiles are not summed into aggregate quantiles. Protection-period p10/p50/p90 use empirical actual/mean ratios, pooled over SKUs. Their held-out coverage is reported, not assumed to be 80%.

All model weather features use forecasts issued the previous day. Purchasing uses only forecasts issued at the start-of-day inventory cutoff (the previous day). Beyond available issued horizons, a seasonal sine/cosine regression uses only weather observed through the issue date, with past mean rainfall. Known synthetic promotion and holiday calendars are assumed announced in advance. Historical order quantities represent requested demand, including unfilled units, not censored shipped sales.

Run A trains 2024-06-01 through 2026-03-31 and scores April-May holdout. This same frozen run remains the detector baseline during June-August, avoiding both emerging-trend absorption and an in-sample replacement bias estimate. Run B trains through 2026-05-31 and supplies June-August purchasing forecasts. Monthly retraining was promised in the original proposal but is not implemented: these two fixed runs are an explicit prototype limitation. LightGBM uses four threads. Issued-weather logic has a versioned cache directory; changed ratio calibration has a separate versioned file.

Protection ratios calibrate on issue dates April 6, 13, and 20 (outcomes through May 6). Held-out protection intervals use May 12 and 16 (outcomes through June 1, max L+R=16). The low-sugar ramp is exactly zero at its June 1 onset. Daily WAPE is absolute error sum divided by actual units sum. Protection WAPE uses SKU totals over each supplier's L+R window. No hyperparameters were chosen from held-out planted-event results.

## Detection and explanation

CUSUM monitors attribute shares using requested units. April-May residual mean and standard deviation are estimated out of sample. Reference k=0.25 and a block-bootstrap threshold target nominal ARL=90 days. Bootstrap p-values are approximate; chart dependence and repeated daily testing preclude an exact FDR or ARL guarantee. BH gates both directions across the complete daily chart family. Raw threshold alarms and actual BH-gated exposed alarms, misses, and null-world episodes are separately reported. The weekly baseline is a raw weekly threshold comparison; its rate calibration is also approximate.

The null world preserves known weather (including heat) and normal promotion response while removing preference ramps, exceptional promotion response, and bulk anomalies. It is an empirical stress test, not a mathematical null guarantee. Weak pooled district reviews have correlated inputs and uncalibrated pooled thresholds; they remain watch items and never change default purchasing factors.

Clustering has deterministic ordering and merges overlapping SKU drivers. Investigator findings match offline events only with compatible observation dates, affected districts, direction, and SKU scope. Unmatched findings are counted. Matched-label accuracy alone is not detector recall; missed trends are reported separately. Event windows allow 14 days of residual post-event alarm persistence, explicitly affecting the matching denominator. Concentration, promotion, exceptional heat, own-unit change, store spread, and recent persistence are heuristic evidence checks. Their confidence scores are rule weights, not calibrated probabilities.

## Purchasing, capacity, and replay

Inventory is the previous day's closing on-hand plus outstanding purchase orders. Suggestions use the next supplier order weekday. Demand coverage starts on the plan day and ends after waiting-to-order + lead time + review days, so the waiting period is included in both target units and displayed coverage. The weather issue date remains the original cutoff even for future supplier order days. In replay, ordering uses start-of-day inventory and data through yesterday; shipping today's demand then follows. Each policy receives identical demand, starting inventory, inherited open POs (including their fixed arrival schedule), and deterministic supplier mean lead times for new orders. Live historical-plan capacity uses supplier expected dates, not future realized receipt dates. Replay freezes the inherited arrival schedule identically across policies; this is an explicit offline assumption. Historical-policy replay is a comparison rule, not exact recreation of the original stochastic historical simulator.

New orders are case-rounded and either at least MOQ or zero. Capacity allocation is a marginal-value-per-pallet removal heuristic, not an optimum. It projects existing stock on each expected open-PO/new-order arrival date, subtracting only available issued-forecast demand from the inherited stock. Incoming suggested cases remain reserved for the entire projection horizon (a conservative choice); their later shipments do not release forecast capacity. This avoids assuming all pipeline arrives before any consumption. The horizon includes the latest existing/new arrival. Each day has its own capacity ceiling; unavoidable inherited excess on one day never creates extra space on another day. Inventory already committed above capacity is reported separately. Forecast demand is uncertain, so realized morning occupancy can exceed this projected limit; replay reports this honestly. A joint probabilistic occupancy model remains an improvement.

Replay reports both end-of-day average/maximum pallets and peak/day-count before shipping, so morning arrival pressure is visible. Historical baselines are unconstrained, making capacity/fill tradeoffs essential when comparing policies. The report does not claim Scoop beats every historical buffer.

Default directions adjust only significant findings classified persistent. One-offs, weather, data issues, mirrors, uncertain labels, and pooled weak signals stay neutral until the planner changes decisions. Margin uses SKU-specific economic critical ratios; fill-rate and sales goals use 97% and 98% protection demand quantiles respectively unless an explicit service level overrides them. These are planning quantiles, not guaranteed achieved fill rates. A planner may target a SKU or a tag within districts. Confirmation rechecks the dated occupancy path with persisted directions and manual case overrides. Existing plans cannot be recreated destructively, and watch identifiers are stable SHA-256 prefixes.

## Remaining limitations

No expiry, supplier variability for new replay orders, transfer network, store inventory, explicit markdown salvage, live feeds, or production deployment is modeled. Ratios pool heterogeneous SKUs and overlap within the calibration set, although validation is separated in time. Calendar-wait windows can be longer than the calibrated L+R windows, so displayed protection intervals for those suggestions are extrapolated. Supplier mean lead times in replay remove delay risk. Conservative reservation and uncertain demand can reduce fill or yield realized overcapacity despite projected feasibility. Numerical results must be read with these limitations and the synthetic seed 42 context.


## Final application semantics

Plan headline metrics use the selected plan date, while operational watches follow observed history through the earlier of the current demo date and the end of that plan week. Stockout and excess watches count individual SKUs against supplier lead time and twice (lead time + 7 days), respectively, using trailing seven-day demand. Zero-demand stock is excess. Tag-share charts compare the monitored actual and expected district shares, and are labelled for review rather than assigned a new uncalibrated significance claim. Generated watches are refreshed at strategy approval; user/chat watches and archived records remain.

The arrival projection uses the plan date and all current proposed/confirmed quantities. It is a frozen forecast; user-confirmed synthetic purchase orders do not enter historical stock or historical pipeline a second time. The 35 C scenario overrides temperature only during the requested week, preserving rainfall and all information cutoffs. Neither scenario analysis nor reading a watch applies purchases.
